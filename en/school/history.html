Annales Scholae Coronensis

History

From the parish school recorded in 1388 to Honterus’s humanist gymnasium and today’s national college.

  1. Origins1235–1388
  2. The age of Honterus1498–1549
  3. The Saxon gymnasium1689–1903
  4. The twentieth century1940–1980
  5. Today1990–

The college takes 1541 as its founding year: that is when building began on the new school under Johannes Honterus. The town school itself is older – its first named schoolman appears in 1388 – and the constitution of 1543 and the first register of 1544 have also served as reference points. The 300th and 400th anniversaries (1845 and 1944) were counted from 1544.

The school’s names in modern times: Honterus-Gymnasium (1903–1948), “Liceul mixt german” (a co-educational German-language lyceum, after 1948), “Johannes Honterus” General Lyceum (1971–1977), lyceum of philology and history with a mathematics and physics section (1977–1990), “Johannes Honterus” Theoretical Lyceum (1990–2019) and, since 2019, the “Johannes Honterus” National College.

Origins

  1. 1235

    A convent “in Corona”

    According to the school chronicle, the convent “claustrum sororum in Corona” is first recorded in this year, on the site of today’s Building B. Corona is the Latin name of Brașov.

    1Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— A convent “in Corona” (opens an external site)

  2. 1388

    The school is first recorded

    Theodoricus, “formerly scolarium succentor in Corona” – assistant cantor of the Brașov school – is the first schoolman known by name. A plaque placed in 2021 marks the site of the first parish school, 1388–1547.

    2Source · honterusgemeinde.ro— The school is first recorded (opens an external site)

The age of Honterus

  1. 1498

    Johannes Honterus is born

    Johannes Honterus (originally Austen) is born in Brașov, the son of a prosperous tanner. His Latinised name comes from the Transylvanian Saxon “Hontert”, meaning elder (the shrub).

    3Source · deutsche-biographie.de— Johannes Honterus is born (opens an external site)

  2. 1520–1525

    Studies in Vienna

    He enrols at the University of Vienna as “Johannes Aust ex Corona”, becoming bachelor in 1522 and magister artium in 1525.

    4Source · kulturstiftung.org— Studies in Vienna (opens an external site)

  3. 1532

    The first printed map of Transylvania

    In Basel, where he works as a woodcutter, Honterus publishes the “Chorographia Transylvaniae – Sybembürgen”, signed with the monogram J.H.C., and two star charts after Albrecht Dürer.

    5Source · mek.oszk.hu— The first printed map of Transylvania (opens an external site)

  4. 1533

    Return to Brașov

    Called back by the town council, Honterus returns to Brașov to reorganise its schools. From 1536 he sits on the council.

    6Source · deutsche-biographie.de— Return to Brașov (opens an external site)

  5. 1539

    The Honterus press

    Honterus’s press begins work and prints the first school textbooks for the trivium. It is the first lasting press in Transylvania; its mark is Brașov’s crown-and-roots arms.

    7Source · typographia.oszk.hu— The Honterus press (opens an external site)

  6. 1541

    Honterus’s new school

    On 26 April 1541 building begins on a new school, with a public lecture hall, on the site of the former St Catherine’s convent. The college celebrates 3 October 1541 as its founding day.

    8Source · forumkronstadt.ro— Honterus’s new school (opens an external site)

  7. 1542

    Rudimenta Cosmographica and the first school play

    The final version of the “Rudimenta Cosmographica” appears in Brașov: world geography in 1,366 hexameters, with an atlas of woodcut maps. The pupils give a public play in the Town Hall.

    9Source · kulturstiftung.org— Rudimenta Cosmographica and the first school play (opens an external site)

  8. 1543

    Constitutio Scholae Coronensis

    Honterus gives the school its own constitution, the first school regulation in the country. It founds the Coetus, the community of senior pupils, who elect their own officers and take responsibility for school life.

    10Source · siebenbuerger.de— Constitutio Scholae Coronensis (opens an external site)

  9. 1544

    The first school register

    Rector Valentin Wagner, Honterus’s collaborator, opens the school register, kept until 1810. On 22 April the same year Honterus is elected town pastor.

    11Source · kulturstiftung.org— The first school register (opens an external site)

  10. 1547

    The library building

    The library building, the “Bibliotheca Scholae Coronensis”, rises on the site of the old school, with the library upstairs and the lecture hall below. A 2021 plaque marks the spot opposite the Black Church’s west portal.

    12Source · honterusgemeinde.ro— The library building (opens an external site)

  11. 23.01.1549

    Death of Honterus

    Johannes Honterus dies in Brașov on 23 January 1549 and is buried in the choir of the Black Church.

    13Source · deutsche-biographie.de— Death of Honterus (opens an external site)

The Saxon gymnasium

  1. 1689

    The great fire

    On 21 April 1689 the city fire destroys the famous school library; the school chronicle records that only 26 volumes survived. The parish church burns too, and its blackened walls give it the name Black Church.

    14Source · de.wikipedia.org— The great fire (opens an external site)

  2. 1743–1748

    The gymnasium is rebuilt

    The gymnasium building, today’s Building B, is rebuilt from the ground up: foundation stone on 22 May 1743, pupils move in in December 1744, work complete in 1748.

    15Source · adz.news— The gymnasium is rebuilt (opens an external site)

  3. 1822–1823

    The boys’ school

    According to the school chronicle, Building A is built in 1822–23 as the boys’ school. Building C had gone up in 1793 to house the school library.

    16Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— The boys’ school (opens an external site)

  4. 1845

    The first Honterusfest

    The first Honterusfest is held for the school’s 300th anniversary. Joseph Dück publishes his “Geschichte des Kronstädter Gymnasiums”, a history of the school.

    17Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— The first Honterusfest (opens an external site)

  5. 1898

    The Honterus statue

    On 21 August 1898, the 400th anniversary of Honterus’s birth, the bronze statue by the Berlin sculptor Harro Magnussen is unveiled in the churchyard.

    18Source · de.wikipedia.org— The Honterus statue (opens an external site)

  6. 1903

    The Honterus-Gymnasium

    The Evangelical gymnasium in Brașov officially takes the name “Honterus-Gymnasium”.

    19Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— The Honterus-Gymnasium (opens an external site)

The twentieth century

  1. 1940/41

    The end of the Coetus

    The 1938/39 school year is the Coetus’s last regular year. After October 1940, under the political pressure of the time, the student body effectively ceases to exist.

    20Source · siebenbuerger.de— The end of the Coetus (opens an external site)

  2. 1948

    Nationalisation

    The education reform nationalises the school, which loses its name and buildings. According to the school chronicle, German-language teaching continues at a state school, first as the “Liceul mixt german” (a co-educational German-language lyceum).

    21Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— Nationalisation (opens an external site)

  3. 1970–1971

    The Honterus name returns

    In 1970 General School No. 4 takes the name “Johannes Honterus”; in 1971 the “Johannes Honterus” General Lyceum is founded and assigned Buildings A, B and C.

    22Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— The Honterus name returns (opens an external site)

  4. 1980

    Honterusschule heute

    The first school newspaper, “Honterusschule heute”, appears.

    23Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— Honterusschule heute (opens an external site)

Today

  1. 1990

    “Johannes Honterus” Theoretical Lyceum

    The school becomes a theoretical lyceum. In the following years Buildings C (1991) and B (1994–96) are renovated with support from Austria and Germany.

    24Source · liceulhonterusbrasov.ro— “Johannes Honterus” Theoretical Lyceum (opens an external site)

  2. 1995

    Deutsches Sprachdiplom

    According to the Allgemeine Deutsche Zeitung, the school has held the examination for the German Language Diploma (DSD) of the Kultusministerkonferenz since 1995.

    25Source · adz.news— Deutsches Sprachdiplom (opens an external site)

  3. 2019

    National College

    On 1 September 2019, by ministerial order, the lyceum becomes the “Johannes Honterus” National College. Since 2020 it has belonged to the Alliance of Centenary Colleges.

    26Source · bizbrasov.ro— National College (opens an external site)

  4. 2021

    Honterus 480

    The school marks 480 years. Two Latin plaques mark the sites of the first parish school (1388–1547) and of the 1547 library.

    27Source · honterusgemeinde.ro— Honterus 480 (opens an external site)
